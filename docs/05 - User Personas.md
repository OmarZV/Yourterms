# 05 - User Personas

## Executive Summary

Yourterms is designed for self-directed investors who want to make their own investment decisions but often lack the tools to evaluate the behavioral forces influencing those decisions.

Traditional investing personas are frequently segmented by age, income, assets, or investing experience.

Yourterms takes a different approach.

Because the product focuses on **decision quality**, its users are primarily differentiated by:

- How they make investment decisions
- Where they struggle
- What creates uncertainty
- Which behavioral biases affect them
- How much guidance they want
- How much control they want to retain

Four initial personas represent the strongest opportunities for Yourterms.

---

| Persona | Primary Need | Behavioral Challenge | Yourterms Opportunity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Developing Investor** | Confidence & education | Information overload | Explain and contextualize |

# Primary Persona

## Persona 1 — The Developing Investor

> **"I understand the basics of investing, but I'm never completely sure whether I'm making the right decision."**

### Profile

The Developing Investor has moved beyond simply saving money and has begun actively managing investments.

They understand basic concepts such as:

- Stocks
- ETFs
- Diversification
- Risk
- Long-term investing

However, when markets become volatile or an individual investment moves significantly, they struggle to determine what information actually matters.

### Experience Level

**Beginner → Intermediate**

### Goals

- Build long-term wealth
- Become a more knowledgeable investor
- Make decisions independently
- Avoid major investing mistakes
- Become more confident evaluating investments

### Behaviors

The Developing Investor frequently:

- Searches Google before making investment decisions
- Reads financial news
- Watches investment content
- Checks brokerage accounts frequently
- Looks at analyst ratings
- Asks friends or online communities for opinions
- Uses AI tools to explain financial concepts

### Pain Points

- Too much conflicting information
- Difficulty separating useful information from noise
- Uncertainty about whether to buy, sell, or hold
- Financial terminology can be intimidating
- Doesn't always understand why an investment moved
- Has difficulty connecting market information to personal goals

### Behavioral Risks

Most susceptible to:

- **Herd behavior**
- **Recency bias**
- **Anchoring**
- **Loss aversion**

### Current Alternatives

The investor may currently combine:

- Brokerage research
- Financial media
- Search engines
- Reddit / social media
- YouTube
- General-purpose AI assistants

The experience is fragmented across multiple products.

### Yourterms Opportunity

Yourterms can become the investor's **decision companion**.

Rather than simply presenting more information, the product can help the investor understand:

1. What information matters
2. What information may not matter
3. What assumptions they are making
4. What behavioral biases may be present
5. How the decision relates to their goals

### Jobs To Be Done

> **When I'm considering an investment decision, help me understand what matters so I can make the decision confidently myself.**

### Example Prompt

> "Nvidia has fallen 20% and I'm thinking about buying more. What should I think about before doing it?"

### Product Value

**Complexity → Understanding → Confidence**

# Persona 2 — The Emotional Investor

> **"I know I shouldn't react emotionally, but when the market starts moving, it's difficult not to."**

### Profile

The Emotional Investor understands investing principles but struggles to consistently follow them when markets become volatile.

Their investment strategy may be rational when markets are calm.

Their behavior changes when money is being gained or lost.

### Experience Level

**Beginner → Experienced**

Investment experience does not necessarily eliminate behavioral bias.

### Goals

- Avoid emotional investment decisions
- Remain disciplined during volatility
- Stick to a long-term strategy
- Reduce regret
- Stop constantly reacting to markets

### Behaviors

The Emotional Investor may:

- Check portfolio performance frequently
- Become anxious during market declines
- Chase investments after large gains
- Sell after significant losses
- Change strategies frequently
- Follow market sentiment closely

### Pain Points

- Knows what they "should" do but struggles to follow through
- Regrets impulsive investment decisions
- Finds market volatility stressful
- Has difficulty separating emotion from analysis
- Recognizes behavioral mistakes only after they happen

### Behavioral Risks

Most susceptible to:

- **Loss aversion**
- **FOMO**
- **Herd behavior**
- **Recency bias**
- **Disposition effect**

### Yourterms Opportunity

Yourterms can introduce **intelligent friction** between emotional reaction and financial action.

Before making a major decision, the product can ask:

> "What changed about your investment thesis?"

> "Would you make the same decision if the market had not fallen today?"

> "How does this decision align with the time horizon you originally selected?"

### Jobs To Be Done

> **When markets make me want to react immediately, help me slow down and determine whether something meaningful has actually changed.**

### Example Prompt

> "The market is crashing. Should I sell everything before it gets worse?"

### Product Value

**Reaction → Reflection → Discipline**

# Persona 3 — The Independent Researcher

> **"I don't want someone to tell me what to buy. I want someone to challenge my thinking."**

### Profile

The Independent Researcher is comfortable analyzing investments and prefers maintaining control over their portfolio.

They may read:

- Earnings reports
- Financial statements
- Analyst research
- Industry news
- Investor presentations
- Market commentary

Their problem is not access to information.

Their problem is evaluating whether their own interpretation of that information is sufficiently objective.

### Experience Level

**Intermediate → Advanced**

### Goals

- Make stronger investment decisions
- Pressure-test investment theses
- Identify risks they may have overlooked
- Reduce confirmation bias
- Maintain independent control

### Behaviors

The Independent Researcher:

- Conducts substantial research before investing
- Builds personal investment theses
- Follows specific companies closely
- Compares valuation metrics
- Holds investments for longer periods
- Has strong opinions about certain investments

### Pain Points

- Research is time consuming
- Difficult to recognize personal blind spots
- Easy to find evidence supporting an existing thesis
- Existing tools provide information rather than intellectual challenge
- General-purpose AI can be overly agreeable

### Behavioral Risks

Most susceptible to:

- **Confirmation bias**
- **Overconfidence**
- **Anchoring**
- **Sunk-cost fallacy**

### Yourterms Opportunity

Yourterms becomes an **AI devil's advocate**.

Rather than telling the investor whether their thesis is correct, the system actively searches for weaknesses.

For example:

> "You believe margins will expand over the next three years. What happens to your thesis if margins remain flat?"

Or:

> "Here are three arguments that challenge your current thesis."

### Jobs To Be Done

> **When I develop an investment thesis, help me identify what I might be missing before I commit capital.**

### Example Prompt

> "I think this stock is dramatically undervalued. Challenge my thesis."

### Product Value

**Conviction → Challenge → Stronger Thesis**

# Persona 4 — The Portfolio Optimizer

> **"I track my portfolio performance. I want to understand whether I'm actually becoming a better investor."**

### Profile

The Portfolio Optimizer has invested for several years and increasingly wants to understand their own decision-making patterns.

Traditional brokerage analytics tell them:

- Portfolio return
- Gains and losses
- Asset allocation
- Dividend income
- Performance over time

But these metrics don't explain **why the investor made particular decisions**.

### Experience Level

**Intermediate → Advanced**

### Goals

- Improve investing discipline
- Understand recurring behavioral patterns
- Evaluate past decisions
- Separate luck from decision quality
- Become a better investor over time

### Behaviors

The Portfolio Optimizer may:

- Track performance carefully
- Maintain watchlists
- Review previous trades
- Compare performance against benchmarks
- Maintain investment notes
- Analyze portfolio allocation

### Pain Points

Existing platforms can answer:

> "What happened?"

But rarely:

> "Why did you make that decision?"

The investor lacks a structured record of their reasoning at the time decisions were made.

### Behavioral Risks

Potential biases include:

- **Overconfidence**
- **Hindsight bias**
- **Disposition effect**
- **Confirmation bias**

### Yourterms Opportunity

Yourterms can create a **Behavioral Decision Journal**.

Each significant investment decision can record:

- Decision
- Reasoning
- Confidence
- Expected outcome
- Time horizon
- Risks identified
- Behavioral signals
- Actual outcome

Over time, AI can identify patterns across decisions.

For example:

> "You have considered selling during five significant market declines. Four times, your underlying investment thesis had not changed."

### Jobs To Be Done

> **When I review my investment history, help me understand how I make decisions so I can improve over time.**

### Example Prompt

> "Looking at my decisions over the last year, what mistakes do I repeatedly make?"

### Product Value

**History → Pattern Recognition → Improvement**

# MVP Target User

The strongest initial user sits between the **Developing Investor** and **Emotional Investor**.

They:

- Already invest independently
- Understand basic investing concepts
- Use an online brokerage
- Research investments themselves
- Want to retain control of their decisions
- Occasionally struggle with uncertainty or emotion
- Do not necessarily need a financial advisor
- Want more personalized guidance than existing tools provide

### MVP User Statement

> **A self-directed investor who knows enough to make their own decisions but wants help making those decisions more thoughtfully.**

---

# Key User Tension

Yourterms exists between two existing solutions.

### Fully Self-Directed

> "Give me the information and I'll figure it out."

### Delegated Advice

> "Tell me what to do or manage my portfolio for me."

### Yourterms

> **"Help me think through the decision so I can decide for myself."**

This distinction is central to the product's positioning.

---

# Cross-Persona Needs

Despite differences in experience and behavior, the personas share several needs.

### Personalization

Guidance should account for the investor's goals, portfolio, time horizon, and previous decisions.

### Explainability

Users should understand why the AI surfaced an insight.

### Independence

Users retain control over investment decisions.

### Intellectual Challenge

The product should challenge assumptions rather than simply validate them.

### Trust

The AI must distinguish facts, interpretations, assumptions, and uncertainty.

### Reflection

The product should encourage thoughtful decisions rather than impulsive action.

---

# Design Implications

The personas suggest several product requirements.

## 1. Progressive Complexity

The interface should work for developing investors without feeling simplistic to experienced investors.

## 2. Conversational First

Investors should be able to describe decisions naturally rather than navigating complex financial interfaces.

## 3. Behavioral Signals Should Be Contextual

Yourterms should not simply label users:

> "You have confirmation bias."

Instead:

> "Your reasoning currently relies heavily on evidence supporting your existing thesis. Would you like to explore the strongest arguments against it?"

## 4. User Control Is Critical

Yourterms should help investors evaluate decisions without becoming an automated portfolio manager.

## 5. Personalization Should Improve Over Time

The product should become increasingly useful as it learns:

- Goals
- Investment philosophy
- Previous decisions
- Behavioral patterns
- Risk preferences
- Recurring areas of uncertainty

---

# Key Persona Insight

The target user does not necessarily need **more financial information**.

They need help interpreting information within the context of:

**their portfolio + their goals + their behavior + their decision.**

This creates the central personalization opportunity for Yourterms.

---

# Product Thesis

> **The most valuable investing assistant may not be the one that knows the most about the market, but the one that understands both the market and the investor making the decision.**
